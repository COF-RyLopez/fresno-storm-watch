Return to the map

Terrain and FEMA reference are connected; photorealistic imagery still needs administrator enablement and coverage checks. Setup and coverage checks. From a river signal to a prepositioning review

The river view brings observed conditions, published forecasts and information gaps together. It supports an EOC review of where attention may be needed. Staging locations still need an asset owner, access assessment and operational decision.

  1. Find a watchpoint. Review Fresno County’s San Joaquin boundary reaches, Kings River sites, Little Dry Creek and James Bypass. These nine candidates are not a complete inventory of rivers, irrigation channels or gates. Stations monitoring the San Joaquin boundary reach remain included even where the publisher labels their county Madera. Kaweah at McKay Point, Eastside Bypass at El Nido and Chowchilla Bypass at Ripperdan are excluded from this county-focused list.
  2. Read the evidence. Compare the observation time, recent measured change, forecast issue time, highest sampled forecast and the forecast's end time. A repeating high is not a precisely timed crest.
  3. Inspect FEMA vicinity. Open gauge details and use the map button. At local zoom, the app shows selected FEMA mapped hazard areas. Click a polygon for its zone and study identifiers. FEMA's hazard area is not generated from the gauge forecast.
  4. Connect local consequences. Add county facilities, communities, bridge approaches, closure points and designated staging sites. Link them to a documented river reach or reviewed flood scenario. A gauge proximity radius alone does not establish exposure.
  5. Review with the responsible team. Confirm release plans, levee and gate conditions, access routes, action thresholds and available resources with NWS and local operators. Record the decision and assignment through the approved EOC workflow.

What each cue means

  • Threshold cue: a current observation or usable future sample meets a category actually supplied by NWPS for that gauge. A forecast sample brackets an exceedance; the app does not interpolate an exact crossing time. None of the selected live stations supplied usable categories during the October 17, 2026 check.
  • Forecast higher: the highest future sample is above the current observed value in comparable units. This is a review cue, not a flood category, probability or county action trigger. Forecast and observed baselines can differ.
  • Data gap: observation missing, old, source-marked not current, or query failed. A failed station retains its previous values with an explicit warning.
  • Monitor source: valid observations are available without a higher-priority cue. It does not mean no flood risk. Unknown thresholds and absent forecasts remain visible.

Observed values older than two hours and forecasts issued more than 24 hours ago are marked unsuitable for current planning cues. These are configurable prototype review ages, not publisher guarantees. Forecasts are never extended past their last sample. Sample timestamps, forecast issuance and application retrieval are separate.

All gauge stages are relative to the individual gauge datum. They cannot be subtracted from a terrain elevation or another gauge's stage without surveyed reference and datum reconciliation. Missing sentinels are not plotted as zero. Native units remain visible; threshold comparisons use only explicit conversions within the same dimension.

FEMA flood zones are a reference

The app reads FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer, Flood Hazard Zones directly. It draws special flood hazard areas, mapped 0.2% annual-chance areas and undetermined Zone D that intersect Fresno County. This is a client-side display filter, not a geometry clip: a retained polygon may extend across the county boundary. Do not use the display filter as an asset-exposure calculation. Other zone categories are filtered out. Floodway detail and effective map documents must be checked in the FEMA Map Service Center. An unshaded area is not a finding of safety or confirmed complete coverage.

A FEMA overlay identifies mapped susceptibility. An NWPS forecast describes a point on a river. Converting that forecast into affected roads, buildings or a moving flood extent requires an applicable reviewed inundation library or hydraulic model, including datum, levee and operational assumptions. Those results are not yet in this app.

If the overlay says FEMA unavailable, treat coverage as unknown and use the official FEMA maps link. During the October 17 prototype checks the service rendered polygons, then later failed to load. Reloading can retry the source, but a successful reload is not guaranteed. The live river feed operates independently.

What the EOC needs next

  • Planning: named river reaches and communities, time windows, NWS coordination and approved action thresholds with units, datum, owner and effective date.
  • Operations: closures, field observations and a link from each watch area to its responsible team.
  • Logistics: approved staging facilities, resource availability, access restrictions and lead times.
  • GIS: public/private item links and owners for facilities, bridges, channels, gates, levees and reviewed inundation scenarios.

The prototype presents one shared public-source view. Role-specific views, private assets, shared review records and task assignments still require authenticated services and the county's access model. The separate Experience Builder pilot is retained.

3D terrain and camera conditions

Use 3D terrain to view the same gauges, cameras and FEMA zones over Esri World Terrain. Overhead and Tilt terrain reset orientation; 2D returns to the map. Imagery adds historical aerial/satellite context, not a live scene. Only one view renders at a time. If 3D cannot initialize, the previous view and independent data panels remain available.

The terrain service reports orthometric elevations in meters compiled from multiple sources with varying resolution. The display has no vertical exaggeration. Gauges and cameras use ground-relative map symbols, not measured tower or water heights. FEMA polygons drape on the terrain; there is no modeled water surface, depth, levee analysis, surveyed bridge clearance, camera field of view or safe staging recommendation. Do not subtract gauge stage from this terrain.

The River-area cameras panel reads the Caltrans District 6 feed directly. Eight highway cameras are selected for SR-41 and SR-99 San Joaquin approaches, the SR-99 Kings River crossing near Kingsburg, and SR-180 Reed Avenue near the Kings River. Madera and Tulare records remain labeled as boundary approaches. Their location does not prove they can see the river. Select a camera, use Locate on map, then compare the map with its image. The SR-41 cameras also link to the Highway 41 gauge.

Still image: one selected camera refreshes while the panel and tab are visible, no faster than the publisher's interval or once per minute. “Still file updated” comes from the HTTP Last-Modified header and is only a freshness proxy. “Still loaded in app” is a different time. Capture time is not supplied separately; inspect the image's timestamp overlay. A recent load does not make an old image current. Unknown/future file times and failed updates remain apparent. The prototype marks a file old after the greater of five minutes or three published image intervals.

Live video: press Play live video to request the publisher's HLS stream. Latency varies. A stalled, ended or failed stream returns to still-image view; the app does not call a frozen frame live. Changing cameras, closing the panel or hiding the tab stops playback. No video starts automatically when returning to a tab. The bundled hls.js player is pinned to 1.7.3 and its license is included. Browser codec support and agency availability can differ.

At the October 17 visual check, five selected still images showed road conditions. Valley Children’s Boulevard, SR-99 Herndon Avenue and SR-99 Route 201/Kingsburg served outage graphics despite reporting in service. Those entries carry a dated review warning and crossed camera marker. This is a manual finding, not automatic image-content monitoring; a maintainer must recheck before clearing it. HTTP success and a recent file time alone do not verify camera content.

Coverage gaps: this initial selection has no camera at Friant Dam, James Bypass, Mendota or the upstream watchpoints. It is not a complete inventory of public or county cameras. County-owned cameras can be added later with the owner's permitted viewing URL, coordinates, update method, access classification and verified field of view. Public code must not include private camera credentials.

The catalog is checked every five minutes while visible; its record date describes catalog changes, not image capture. Unknown/out-of-service cameras, changed identities/locations, failed catalog checks and overdue checks disable new media requests. The curated selection and source register document inclusion and refresh rules. Caltrans QuickMap remains available independently. Caltrans conditions of use apply. This app does not archive images or video.

Weather, rainfall forecasts and reservoir controls

Open Weather & reservoirs, then choose Radar or Rainfall forecast. One atmospheric overlay is displayed at a time so an observation cannot be confused with a forecast. Gauges, reservoir locations, cameras and the FEMA reference remain available in both 2D and 3D. Sierra & river approaches fits an upstream weather context rectangle; it is not a delineated catchment or an expansion of the Fresno watchlist.

Radar: the NOAA MRMS radar reflectivity image service supplies time-tagged images. The app requests CONUS records only and locks the image to the selected raster ID. Latest available follows new frames; selecting history holds an older frame until the operator changes it. When that frame leaves the publisher archive, the overlay is hidden and the app asks for Latest rather than silently substituting another time. The service advertises five-minute updates and a moving archive; the actual returned frame times control this app. A latest frame older than 20 minutes, failed check or check older than seven minutes hides the overlay. These are prototype review ages. Echoes are reflectivity (dBZ), not rainfall amounts. The publisher serves a rendered RGB image, so this app provides a qualitative echo-color key and links the official NWS numerical legend; it does not infer a pixel's dBZ or rainfall rate. Missing coverage, beam blockage and non-rain echoes remain possible.

Precipitation forecast: WPC QPF supplies 24-hour Day 1, 72-hour Days 1–3 and 168-hour Days 1–7 products (layers 1, 9 and 11). The app verifies a single issue and valid period across all returned band records, then filters the drawing to that cycle. These are fixed WPC periods, not rolling windows from the present. Source wall-clock fields are UTC and displayed in local Pacific time. QPF measures liquid equivalent, including melted snow/ice; it is not a snowfall depth, runoff prediction, basin average or flood extent. A forecast older than 24 hours, ended valid period, failed check or check older than seven minutes hides it. A cycle change closes an old popup.

Rainfall colors use the same six sequential blue groups for every period: 0.01–<0.25, 0.25–<0.50, 0.50–<1, 1–<2, 2–<5 and 5+ inches of the source polygon value. Click a polygon for its original value and UTC dates. The app does not integrate these polygons over a basin. Unshaded areas are not assumed to be zero. Radar retains the publisher's colors; the rainfall legend is an app classification with fixed breaks. Map display uses Web Mercator/SceneView; the SDK projects the WPC source CRS without area calculations.

Reservoirs: two initial controls are Millerton/Friant on the San Joaquin and Pine Flat on the Kings. DWR CDEC's public JSON service provides the operator reports. Millerton uses daily elevation, storage, inflow, total outflow, river outlet release and spill. Pine Flat uses hourly elevation, storage, inflow and outflow. CDEC documents all web-service times as Pacific Standard Time year-round (UTC−08:00); hourly readings convert to local Pacific time, while daily report dates stay dates. The elevation datum is not specified in this JSON feed and is not assumed to match terrain or gauge datum.

Millerton sensor 85 is river outlet release; sensor 71 is spillway discharge; sensor 23 is total reservoir outflow. They are displayed separately. Inflows can be computed water-balance values, including negatives, rather than direct measurements. Readings with missing sentinels, wrong units, nonblank quality flags or invalid/future times do not become valid numbers. A newer missing/flagged record is shown alongside the last valid value. Hourly readings older than three hours and daily reports older than 72 hours are marked old; these ages need operator review. No percentage of flood-control space, release target, routed arrival time or staging recommendation is calculated.

Scheduled release notices: CDEC event sensor 7 (REL SCH, cfs) supplies operator-reported schedules for Millerton/Friant and Pine Flat. The collector requests 31 days of preceding records and a seven-day look-ahead. Notices display the planned amount, effective local time and change from the previous valid schedule; they are not measured flows. Issuance time is not supplied. Pine Flat's USACE notices remain an additional operator link. The JSON service's fixed PST times convert to local Pacific time, including daylight saving, and were checked against CDEC's human-readable notice pages. An empty result means no upcoming notices returned within the queried horizon. Unscheduled changes can occur; confirm with the operator.

Feed handling: CDEC's padded and unpadded dates/hours are normalized with calendar validation. Identical records are deduplicated by station, sensor, duration and normalized source time. Conflicting records at the same time are quarantined as unknown; they do not freeze unrelated or later valid readings. Unrecognized timestamps remain a visible gap. Millerton daily queries end at the previous completed PST calendar day so an unfinished daily placeholder does not obscure the latest completed report.

Collection: a small Node collector reads public CDEC series and atomically writes reservoir-latest.json for same-origin browser access. The GitHub Actions workflow collects before each deployment on a 15-minute target schedule and uses the last published snapshot for failure fallback. GitHub can delay or skip scheduled work. The app checks the snapshot each minute while visible and warns after 45 minutes without a recent collection. Observation and schedule failures are independent, with retained values and original success times. A successful download never refreshes measurement age. There is no background emergency notification service or complete reservoir/notice revision archive.

Release and downstream gauge trends

Open Release & gauge trends from Weather & reservoirs, a reservoir card, or the lower San Joaquin/Kings Area watch card. Select three or seven days of history and three or seven days ahead. The collector retrieves eight days of CDEC observations; the browser reuses existing NWPS hydrographs. Millerton's daily river outlet reports are dots at the midpoint of their fixed-PST report dates, with the original date retained in the readout and data table. Pine Flat's hourly reported outflow is a line. Millerton total reservoir outflow is excluded; spill remains a separate reservoir metric.

Millerton can be compared with HWY 41 (SJXC1), HWY 145 (RSBC1), Gravelly Ford (GVAC1), or Mendota (SJMC1). Both charts use the same time axis. Gauge flow is converted from kcfs to cfs where supplied and shares a zero-inclusive scale with release. Gauge stage uses a separate, explicitly labeled ft scale; stages at different gauges are not comparable elevations. Blue shows observations, purple dashed shows published NWS forecasts, and hollow amber diamonds mark planned reservoir releases. Dotted schedule guides on the gauge chart indicate reservoir effective times, not downstream arrival times. Retained stale schedules/forecasts use grey and visible status text. Missing samples and long gaps stay open; no release values are carried forward. Hover or use the arrow keys on a chart to inspect exact samples, or expand its data table.

Pine Flat's chart explicitly shows that no current downstream Kings gauge is connected. The existing NWPS Kings sites are upstream. USGS 11221500 has no current continuous series, and CDEC KGF lists full natural flow and seasonal estimates, which are not a substitute for observed downstream flow. The app links the USACE basin report for operator review. New downstream feeds require an authoritative station, measurement contract and operator-confirmed association.

Changing chart windows does not change the banner's fixed review rules. To test future schedule markers without mixing fictional and live evidence, open the fictional Area watch exercise, choose Scheduled releases, then Release & gauge trends.

Broader upstream storage, snowpack and basin precipitation averages remain later work. Use current NWPS forecasts and NWS Hanford/CNRFC coordination to interpret weather and operator conditions together.

River-area review and possible prepositioning

Scheduled release — review: a valid upcoming notice within seven days, or a notice effective within the past 24 hours, adds a review cue when its amount differs from the previous valid schedule or that previous amount is unknown. Increases and decreases both require review; an unchanged repeated schedule does not inflate counts. Millerton notices apply only to the Friant–Mendota review group; Pine Flat notices apply only to the Kings below Pine Flat. The banner shows the amount and effective time, while Source evidence shows the schedule and official link. Effective time is at the reservoir, not downstream arrival. Old/failed checks and flagged/conflicting notices are excluded from active cues and shown as gaps. Schedule changes use stable station/time IDs and content revisions in the private EOC review queue; a fresh collection alone does not revise a notice. A disappeared notice is not proof of cancellation or safe conditions.

Area watch connects rainfall, reported releases and NWPS gauge changes in six named Fresno river review groups. The persistent banner names areas with current review cues and stays visible when changing work panels. Counts are unique areas; repeated samples and multiple gauges do not multiply them. This is a readiness review queue with explicit rules, not a flood-probability model, suitability score or deployment instruction. Equal categories are sorted alphabetically.

  1. Published NWS threshold cue: a current observed or usable forecast gauge sample meets an NWS action/flood threshold for the same quantity and compatible units. This is listed first. Unknown NWS thresholds stay unknown.
  2. River change — review: a current gauge has a positive observed change over the available 2.5–4-hour comparison, a usable next-72-hour forecast exceeds its current observation, or a current consecutive release report increases. Values and intervals are displayed. Positive differences use display precision (0.01 ft/cfs or 0.001 kcfs for gauges, 1 cfs for releases); these are numerical display rules, not approved rapid-rise or action thresholds. The cue invites verification before considering prepositioning.
  3. Rain + river change — review: a river-change cue and a positive WPC point sample are both available for the same configured review group. This describes co-occurring context. It does not establish causation, travel time, runoff or independent corroboration; river forecasts may already include precipitation and operating assumptions.
  4. Rain forecast — monitor: positive WPC samples alone remain monitoring context. No rainfall amount has been adopted as a local flood-action threshold.
  5. Incomplete evidence: unavailable, old, failed or unverified sources remain explicit. No current cue is not an all-clear.

Fixed rainfall scope: the banner always queries WPC Days 1–3 (72-hour layer 9) at the nine existing gauge points and two CDEC dam points. Each point is sent as EPSG:4326 with an explicit intersect relationship; the service handles its native source CRS. Point queries must match the national product's issue and valid window. The app reports polygon labels and the highest intersecting label where contours overlap or a point lies on a boundary. The displayed maximum is only among that area's configured points. It is not a basin mean, basin maximum, area-weighted total or a runoff estimate. No returned polygon is an unknown amount, never an assumed zero. Changing the displayed map to 24 hours or seven days does not change the banner. Point sampling is not complete upstream precipitation coverage.

Release comparisons: only Millerton river outlet (85) and spill (71), and Pine Flat reported outflow (23), participate. Millerton total reservoir outflow is excluded. Compare consecutive valid reports: 23–25 hours apart for daily MIL reports, 0.5–1.5 hours for hourly PNF reports. Missing intervening records, nonblank flags, changed sensor/units, newer missing records, old readings or failed/overdue collection block increase cues. Last valid readings remain visible as dated evidence. Release values are observations, not announced operating schedules. The existing collection-age and measurement-age rules still apply.

Named areas and associations: San Joaquin Friant–Mendota uses SJXC1, RSBC1, GVAC1 and SJMC1 plus Friant outlet/spill context; Redinger uses RJQC1; Little Dry Creek uses LTDC1; James Bypass uses JAMC1; Kings upstream uses KREC1 and NFCC1; Kings below Pine Flat uses PNF release context. Upstream Kings gauges are not substituted for a downstream gauge. Friant releases are not assigned to Little Dry Creek, Redinger or James Bypass. These are explicit planning groups from the existing source watchlists, not hydrologically routed catchments. Review the association inventory with NWS and operators.

Timing and failures: independent point forecasts check every five minutes while visible, with at most three concurrent requests. Source issue/period, gauge measurement/forecast issue and release report times remain separate. Failed points remain unknown while other successful points can be used. Gauge checks older than seven minutes, observations older than two hours and forecast issues older than 24 hours are not eligible; rainfall also requires a current issue, unexpired window and a check no older than seven minutes. The banner re-evaluates ages every 30 seconds while visible. Reservoir browser checks must be no older than seven minutes and collector success no older than 45 minutes. There is no email/SMS, background paging, team acknowledgement or saved review history in this static prototype.

Operational review: confirm the source change and any planned releases with NWS Hanford/CNRFC and the responsible operator; review field observations, county closures, critical assets and approved staging access. FEMA is a planning reference and is not used as a trigger or current inundation boundary. County action thresholds, equipment needs, lead times, owners and approved staging locations remain unset. Add them only after documented local review; no weighted score or automated resource assignment is implemented.

Exercise: open the fictional area-watch exercise. Try Scheduled releases for future increase/decrease notices, as well as combined rain/rise/release, published threshold, no change, stale and missing cases. These fixtures are isolated from live source callbacks and have no external writes. The separate river-widget exercise controls test that widget independently.

Power outages and Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS)

Open Fresno power / PSPS. The app reads public GIS layers used by the PG&E outage map and the Southern California Edison outage map. These are the utilities’ public map services, not a guaranteed emergency notification API. Source contracts were checked October 18, 2026.

PG&E current-outage points and footprints are combined by outage ID; PG&E PSPS notices remain separate. SCE monitored/downstream circuits mean PSPS consideration; de-energized circuits mean reported power off. “Downstream” here refers to the electrical network. An unrecognized status remains unverified. Other electric utilities, future maintenance schedules and confidential partner/customer records are outside this release.

PG&E checks directly every five minutes while the app is visible. Both utilities also have public snapshots collected by the existing GitHub Actions schedule, targeting 15 minutes; runs and deployment can be delayed. SCE’s service does not allow this app’s browser origin, so it uses those snapshots. The app checks for a new snapshot every minute and marks retrievals older than 45 minutes as overdue. A failed category retains its prior dated data and a failure label. SCE advises that reported outages can take up to 30 minutes to appear on its own map. Retrieval time is not the utility’s record-update time.

The county boundary is the same WGS84 Fresno County boundary used elsewhere in this app. An envelope query is followed by exact point/polygon intersection; county counts stay fixed when the map moves. Utility footprints remain uncut for context and can cross county lines. An outage point may not describe the full service interruption area. Customer estimates are shown per record, never added as a county total: records, circuits and PSPS notices may overlap.

Known epoch/offset-qualified timestamps display in Pacific time with PST/PDT. SCE outage update/estimated-restoration text and any ambiguous PG&E notice strings are shown as publisher text with time-zone uncertainty. Estimated restoration, a weather all-clear, an ended planning window or a record disappearing does not prove restored power. Old record updates, expired estimates, unknown status and failed feeds remain visible for verification.

The banner separates returned outage records, PSPS watch/warning areas and source failures. Changed source status, times, counts or geometry create a change badge. Clearing badges affects this browser tab only, with no saved team acknowledgement or change to utility status. There is no unattended SMS/email or automatic activation, dispatch, evacuation or public messaging. Review backup power, pumping, communications and critical-site conditions with the facility owner and utility liaison.

Use the fictional power exercise to test consideration/shutoff, restoration/cancellation and source failures. Exercise records are kept in memory and never sent to a utility or AGOL. Official address checks and customer notification enrollment remain on the utility websites.

Later cloud-data work

GeoLibre's SQL workspace demonstrates browser-side DuckDB queries and remote file access. For this small live watchlist, direct JSON remains sufficient. For county assets and Overture, the candidate design is GeoParquet for analysis, vector tiles or PMTiles for map drawing, and COGs for applicable rasters. These are candidates, not a claim of measured performance in this app.

Before choosing a large-data engine, obtain data size, projected growth, concurrent users, update frequency, latency targets, access restrictions and an IT owner. Benchmark a bounded DuckDB-WASM read against a managed ArcGIS/PostGIS service: (1) extent filter, (2) asset-to-reviewed-flood-area intersection, and (3) group counts by watch area. Use the same source snapshot and compare feature IDs, distinct asset counts, boundary-touch behavior, CRS and five mapped samples before comparing cold/warm load time, bytes transferred, browser memory and cancellation. A 5k/50k/250k-feature test on the actual EOC computer and a phone is a proposed test matrix, not a completed benchmark.

Remote columnar and tile files need suitable HTTP range responses and CORS. GeoParquet and cloud hosting alone do not guarantee fast rendering. Private data remains behind authorized services; static GitHub files and client-side SQL do not create an access-control boundary. GitHub Actions handles the small public CDEC collection in this release. Faster ingestion, private credentials and shared transactions require appropriately managed infrastructure.
