From a river signal to a prepositioning review

The river view brings observed conditions, published forecasts and information gaps together. It supports an EOC review of where attention may be needed. Staging locations still need an asset owner, access assessment and operational decision.

  1. Find a watchpoint. Review Fresno County’s San Joaquin boundary reaches, Kings River sites, Little Dry Creek and James Bypass. These nine candidates are not a complete inventory of rivers, irrigation channels or gates. Stations monitoring the San Joaquin boundary reach remain included even where the publisher labels their county Madera. Kaweah at McKay Point, Eastside Bypass at El Nido and Chowchilla Bypass at Ripperdan are excluded from this county-focused list.
  2. Read the evidence. Compare the observation time, recent measured change, forecast issue time, highest sampled forecast and the forecast's end time. A repeating high is not a precisely timed crest.
  3. Inspect FEMA vicinity. Open gauge details and use the map button. At local zoom, the app shows selected FEMA mapped hazard areas. Click a polygon for its zone and study identifiers. FEMA's hazard area is not generated from the gauge forecast.
  4. Connect local consequences. Add county facilities, communities, bridge approaches, closure points and designated staging sites. Link them to a documented river reach or reviewed flood scenario. A gauge proximity radius alone does not establish exposure.
  5. Review with the responsible team. Confirm release plans, levee and gate conditions, access routes, action thresholds and available resources with NWS and local operators. Record the decision and assignment through the approved EOC workflow.

What each cue means

  • Threshold cue: a current observation or usable future sample meets a category actually supplied by NWPS for that gauge. A forecast sample brackets an exceedance; the app does not interpolate an exact crossing time. None of the selected live stations supplied usable categories during the October 17, 2026 check.
  • Forecast higher: the highest future sample is above the current observed value in comparable units. This is a review cue, not a flood category, probability or county action trigger. Forecast and observed baselines can differ.
  • Data gap: observation missing, old, source-marked not current, or query failed. A failed station retains its previous values with an explicit warning.
  • Monitor source: valid observations are available without a higher-priority cue. It does not mean no flood risk. Unknown thresholds and absent forecasts remain visible.

Observed values older than two hours and forecasts issued more than 24 hours ago are marked unsuitable for current planning cues. These are configurable prototype review ages, not publisher guarantees. Forecasts are never extended past their last sample. Sample timestamps, forecast issuance and application retrieval are separate.

All gauge stages are relative to the individual gauge datum. They cannot be subtracted from a terrain elevation or another gauge's stage without surveyed reference and datum reconciliation. Missing sentinels are not plotted as zero. Native units remain visible; threshold comparisons use only explicit conversions within the same dimension.

FEMA flood zones are a reference

Other zone categories are filtered out. Floodway detail and effective map documents must be checked in the FEMA Map Service Center. An unshaded area is not a finding of safety or confirmed complete coverage.

A FEMA overlay identifies mapped susceptibility. An NWPS forecast describes a point on a river. Converting that forecast into affected roads, buildings or a moving flood extent requires an applicable reviewed inundation library or hydraulic model, including datum, levee and operational assumptions. Those results are not yet in this app.

If the overlay says FEMA unavailable, treat coverage as unknown and use the official FEMA maps link. During the October 17 prototype checks the service rendered polygons, then later failed to load. Reloading can retry the source, but a successful reload is not guaranteed. The live river feed operates independently.

Shared weather time, GOES and wind forecasts

The weather panel has one planning time: three hours of observation history in 15-minute steps, Now / live, and the next 72 forecast hours. Each source displays its own actual time. Choosing a future time hides radar and GOES observations; the app does not carry an observed image forward as a forecast. WPC precipitation stays a full accumulation window, and is hidden when the selected time is outside that window or predates its update. This timeline does not replay gauges, reservoirs, closures, power records or private EOC records.

GOES: the NOAA nowCOAST satellite WMS supplies GOES East/West infrared and water-vapor imagery. Exact frame times come from each layer’s capabilities; the app selects the nearest earlier frame within 20 minutes and sends that exact UTC time in GetMap. A newest image older than 30 minutes, a failed source check, or a catalog check older than seven minutes hides the image. These are app review ages. Infrared is available day and night; water vapor describes moisture aloft. Neither product measures local surface rainfall or river conditions. The publisher legend is shown without inventing calibrated temperatures for its rendered pixel values.

Wind and gusts: nine named planning points cover San Joaquin Highway 41, Friant/Millerton, Mendota, James Bypass, Pine Flat, Trimmer, Fresno, Coalinga and Shaver Lake. These are selected forecast samples, not a continuous surface or a facility inventory. The official NWS API resolves each point to its forecast grid on every collection. NWS Hanford wind speed, wind gust and wind direction series retain their individual valid intervals and forecast update times. NWS defines wind speed/direction at 10 meters; gusts are the maximum three-second speed within a two-minute interval. See NWS definitions. Values in the verified feed are km/h and are converted to mph using 1 mile = 1.609344 km. Direction is meteorological FROM, degrees relative to true north. Zero is valid; nulls, conflicts and time gaps remain unknown.

The public wind snapshot is collected by the existing Pages workflow on a 15-minute target, not guaranteed. The app checks it every five minutes while visible. If that copy is old, unavailable or incomplete, the open app checks NWS directly, using two concurrent requests and retaining that result in memory for up to 15 minutes. This fallback runs only while the app is open; it is not unattended monitoring. The source status identifies a direct NWS check or scheduled collection and retains each site's success time and errors. Collection age over 45 minutes, a site error, or a forecast update over 24 hours old hides the affected map samples. A new browser check does not renew source issuance. Forecasts at times preceding the latest update are unavailable; this is not an archived forecast replay. The 72-hour wind/gust chart uses hourly step samples of that latest forecast, with gaps preserved. Wind samples can remain visible alongside radar, GOES or rainfall; diamond markers open source-dated details. Labels appear at closer scales to limit clutter.

Wind and gust values provide planning context for field work and power continuity. They do not independently establish utility PSPS decisions, safe access, activation or dispatch thresholds. The weather source record documents publishers, endpoints, units, freshness rules and initial verification. The separate LiDAR terrain preparation and county 3D companion remain available.

Radar: the NOAA MRMS radar reflectivity image service supplies time-tagged images. The app requests CONUS records only and locks the image to the selected raster ID. Now / live follows new frames. The shared timeline holds a selected planning time; radar uses the nearest earlier returned frame within 20 minutes and displays its actual observation time. A future time, missing match or expired archive hides the image. Selecting radar history also moves the shared time. Precipitation forecast: WPC QPF supplies 24-hour Day 1, 72-hour Days 1–3 and 168-hour Days 1–7 products (layers 1, 9 and 11). The app verifies a single issue and valid period across all returned band records, then filters the drawing to that cycle. These are fixed WPC periods, not rolling windows from the present. Source wall-clock fields are UTC and displayed in local Pacific time. QPF measures liquid equivalent, including melted snow/ice; it is not a snowfall depth, runoff prediction, basin average or flood extent. A forecast older than 24 hours, ended valid period, failed check or check older than seven minutes hides it. A cycle change closes an old popup.

Rainfall colors use the same six sequential blue groups for every period: 0.01–<0.25, 0.25–<0.50, 0.50–<1, 1–<2, 2–<5 and 5+ inches of the source polygon value. Click a polygon for its original value and UTC dates. The app does not integrate these polygons over a basin. Unshaded areas are not assumed to be zero. Radar retains the publisher's colors; the rainfall legend is an app classification with fixed breaks. Map display uses Web Mercator/SceneView; the SDK projects the WPC source CRS without area calculations.

Reservoirs: two initial controls are Millerton/Friant on the San Joaquin and Pine Flat on the Kings. DWR CDEC's public JSON service provides the operator reports. Millerton uses daily elevation, storage, inflow, total outflow, river outlet release and spill. Pine Flat uses hourly elevation, storage, inflow and outflow. CDEC documents all web-service times as Pacific Standard Time year-round (UTC−08:00); hourly readings convert to local Pacific time, while daily report dates stay dates. The elevation datum is not specified in this JSON feed and is not assumed to match terrain or gauge datum.

Millerton sensor 85 is river outlet release; sensor 71 is spillway discharge; sensor 23 is total reservoir outflow. They are displayed separately. Inflows can be computed water-balance values, including negatives, rather than direct measurements. Readings with missing sentinels, wrong units, nonblank quality flags or invalid/future times do not become valid numbers. A newer missing/flagged record is shown alongside the last valid value. Hourly readings older than three hours and daily reports older than 72 hours are marked old; these ages need operator review. No percentage of flood-control space, release target, routed arrival time or staging recommendation is calculated.

Scheduled release notices: CDEC event sensor 7 (REL SCH, cfs) supplies operator-reported schedules for Millerton/Friant and Pine Flat. The collector requests 31 days of preceding records and a seven-day look-ahead. Notices display the planned amount, effective local time and change from the previous valid schedule; they are not measured flows. Issuance time is not supplied. Pine Flat's USACE notices remain an additional operator link. The JSON service's fixed PST times convert to local Pacific time, including daylight saving, and were checked against CDEC's human-readable notice pages. An empty result means no upcoming notices returned within the queried horizon. Unscheduled changes can occur; confirm with the operator.

Feed handling: CDEC's padded and unpadded dates/hours are normalized with calendar validation. Identical records are deduplicated by station, sensor, duration and normalized source time. Conflicting records at the same time are quarantined as unknown; they do not freeze unrelated or later valid readings. Unrecognized timestamps remain a visible gap. Millerton daily queries end at the previous completed PST calendar day so an unfinished daily placeholder does not obscure the latest completed report.

Collection: a small Node collector reads public CDEC series and atomically writes reservoir-latest.json for same-origin browser access. The GitHub Actions workflow collects before each deployment on a 15-minute target schedule and uses the last published snapshot for failure fallback. GitHub can delay or skip scheduled work. The app checks the snapshot each minute while visible and warns after 45 minutes without a recent collection. Observation and schedule failures are independent, with retained values and original success times. A successful download never refreshes measurement age. There is no background emergency notification service or complete reservoir/notice revision archive.

How the evacuation traffic was made

Provenance. The cars come from evac-engine, an independent agent-based evacuation model written for this project from the methods published in the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Risk Management Center's LifeSim Technical Reference Manual, version 1.0 (TRM). It is not LifeSim. No run used LifeSim, and evac-engine contains no LifeSim code. Each calculation below cites the TRM section it follows, and every departure from the manual is listed.

  • Speed and traffic density. Dual-regime modified Greenshields model: TRM ch. 7, Computational Inputs, Equations 10 and 11 and Figure 36 (after Mahmassani et al., 2009). Below the breakpoint density a car travels at free-flow speed. Between the breakpoint and jam density, speed follows Equation 11, with the intercept set so speed is continuous at the breakpoint. At or above jam density it is the stop-and-go speed. Density is the vehicles per lane ahead of the car within the look-forward distance. It is counted once per time step and expressed per mile, to match the table units.
  • Road parameters. Lanes, free-flow speed, jam density, breakpoint density, stop-and-go speed and the Greenshields power term come from the CFCC defaults in TRM ch. 7, Road Classification Data, Table 15. OpenStreetMap classes are mapped to the CFCC rows: motorway and trunk to A10–A19, primary to A20–A29, secondary and tertiary to A30–A39, local streets to A40–A49, and ramps to the A50–A68 row. Lane counts tagged in OpenStreetMap replace the default.
  • Evacuation parameters. A 10-second time step, a 1,320 ft look-forward distance, a 20 ft effective vehicle length and a 5-minute live-traffic update interval: the defaults in TRM ch. 7, Evacuation Parameters Defined by Alternative, Table 18.
  • Moving cars each time step. TRM ch. 11, Evacuating With Traffic Simulation (Figure 58) and Caught while Evacuating. Newly mobilized cars are added before density is calculated. Each car moves its speed times the time step across as many road segments as that covers, keeping the same proportional slowdown on each segment. At the start of each segment it reroutes if the next road is flooded. It is caught if no route remains, or if the road it is on floods. With spillback, a segment holding more cars than its lanes and length allow pushes the last car to enter back onto the previous segment.
  • Routing. Fastest paths by Dijkstra's method held in a lookup table, as in TRM ch. 11, Preprocessing the Road Network (Figure 51). The table is rebuilt every live-traffic interval with congested travel times and leaves out roads that will flood before the next rebuild.

Where this differs from the TRM.

  • Willingness to enter a flooded road is sampled per vehicle in LifeSim (TRM ch. 7, Willingness to Enter Flooded Roads, Tables 19 and 20). Here every driver turns back at 0.5 ft.
  • Warning and protective-action timing (TRM chapter 6; TRM ch. 11, Warning Function Sampling) is not modelled. Departures are a placeholder: a warning 15 minutes after the failure begins, then an exponential delay averaging 45 minutes, capped at 6 hours. The stress test compresses those delays to a quarter.
  • LifeSim evacuates to destination points the user places (TRM ch. 7, Destinations). Here the destinations are the first points on major roads and ramps half a mile beyond any mapped water.
  • Not included: vehicle and human stability, fatality rates and life loss, Monte Carlo iterations, pedestrians, the fraction of drivers who reroute in a jam, U-turns, and intersection or signal delay. Each scenario is a single deterministic run. The results are not calibrated against real evacuations.

Data. Flood arrival and maximum depth come from the DSOD-approved inundation maps listed above. Households are the National Structure Inventory buildings inside the 1 ft extent: night-time residents, one car per housing unit, starting at the nearest road node. Roads are from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). The traces record each car's time at every intersection, so cars follow road segments. A road piece is drawn straight between intersections, so cars cut slightly across curved streets.
